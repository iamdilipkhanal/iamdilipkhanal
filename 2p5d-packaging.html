Independent research · ANSYS Mechanical 2026 R1 · October 2026

Thermo-mechanical analysis of a 2.5D chiplet–interposer package.

A documented finite-element research program progressing from analytical thermal-expansion verification to an idealized seven-body global package model and a three-body, displacement-driven local replica. The current study examines how a controlled change in organic-substrate thermal expansion affects global warpage and a selected local stress indicator.

Research status: qualified numerical case study. Verified solver completion and fixed-metric comparisons; no experimental package validation, explicit solder fatigue, or failure-life prediction.

What was actually modelled

The global stack contains seven continuous three-dimensional bodies. Underfill and attachment layers are homogenized elastic surrogates, not individual copper pillars, solder joints, redistribution lines or TSV structures.

Global parent

31,403 structural nodes and 5,421 solid elements in the third-mesh variant used for the controlled comparison.

Local replica

24,087 nodes, 4,950 solid elements; 3D → 3D prescribed UX, UY, UZ on the cut boundary.

Thermal case

Uniform heating from a nominal stress-free 25 °C reference to 125 °C. Constant-property small-strain elastic analysis.

Controlled substrate CTE comparison

Only the organic-substrate expansion coefficient changed: 13.2 → 17.0 ppm/K. A native MAPDL input comparison verified unchanged global mesh, other material coefficients, structural supports and thermal loading. The local model geometry/materials stayed unchanged, with the new global displacement field imported at its cut boundary.

Verification before sensitivity studies

Stage 1 — Free silicon

Analytical αΔTL expansion matched ANSYS (2.3 µm over 10 mm); near-zero free thermal stress.

Stage 2 — Bonded Si–Cu

Two-mesh warpage 53.23939→53.24064 µm; independently derived idealized curvature comparison within ≈1.77% of ANSYS.

Stage 3 — Die–substrate

Sparse Direct fine-mesh Z range ≈111.13 µm; PCG-vs-Direct discrepancy retained as unresolved.

Stage 4 — Interconnect coupon

Axisymmetric Cu/oxide/Si study with 1,430, 5,720 and 22,880 elements; selected fine-to-refined interior metrics passed predefined sensitivity gates.

Stage 5 — 3D global parent

Two-chiplet package; global displacement metrics more mesh-stable than selected peak stress and UF_B source-field vector components.

Stage 6 — 3D local replica

Chosen nonsingular interior path response stable across three local mesh sizes and limited domain/thickness variations; edge maxima not converged.

Stage 7 — Qualified sensitivity

Parent-source comparison and matched-third-mesh organic-substrate CTE perturbation, with recorded global and local response metrics.

Stage 8 — Future independent benchmark

Experiment/literature-matched validation, constitutive uncertainty, and a paper-quality novel contribution have not been completed.

Solver and data integrity

Two completed solves

Global and local ANSYS Sparse Direct solutions finished with zero reported solver errors. An inactive element-shape-check warning remains documented.

4,821 cut nodes

Native imported-load input applies UX, UY and UZ to every one of 4,821 locally mapped cut-boundary nodes; pointwise source-field error remains unquantified.

Preserved study

Versioned Workbench archive contains both Mechanical models and their saved result files. Detailed private technical logs are retained for review.

Interpretation boundary. Parent in-plane source-mesh sensitivity, local edge-stress singularity/mesh response, ideal bonds, inactive element-shape checking and simplified material assumptions restrict extrapolation. This is not a measured reliability study or a validated fatigue-life model.

Future research direction

Develop physically grounded global-to-local interconnect models with material-specific, temperature-dependent nonlinear behavior, appropriate thermal cycling and independent benchmarks. The current elastic case provides a documented starting point, not a substitute for those steps.
